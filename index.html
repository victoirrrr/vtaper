The week tap a day

Cricket is your functional day. Bowling is repeated sprinting, rotation and explosive effort — you already do it weekly. A separate functional session would duplicate it and cost you the rest day.
Jumps and agility are distributed, not isolated. Ten minutes at the start of each strength day, while you're fresh. That's when plyometrics actually build athleticism — done tired at the end of a session, they're just fatigue.

P
Athletic primer — jumps10 minJump
a. Ankle pogo hops — 3 × 10. Stay stiff at the knee, bounce off the balls of your feet, minimal ground time.
b. Box jump — 3 × 5. Box at knee height to begin. Land softly in a quarter squat. Step down, never jump down.
c. Lateral bound — 3 × 6/side. Push off one leg, land on the other, hold the landing 1 full second before the next.

Conversational pace — you should be able to speak full sentences. If you can't, slow down. This builds the aerobic base that carries a bowling spell and a toddler chase.

Sunday · Mobility & recovery

Hold each stretch 30–45 seconds, breathing normally. Mild tension, never pain. No bouncing. Whole thing takes 18–20 min. Do it after your walk, when you're warm.
1
Cat–Cow8 slow reps
On hands and knees. Arch and round the spine slowly. Wakes the spine up before anything else.
2
World's Greatest Stretch5 / side
Lunge forward, left foot outside left hand. Drop the right knee. Rotate left arm up to the ceiling, follow the hand with your eyes. Hold 3 s at the top. Hip flexor, hamstring and thoracic rotation in one.
3
Open Book8 / sideBowling
Lie on your side, knees bent 90°, arms straight out in front, stacked. Keep knees down and rotate the top arm across to the floor behind you. Follow with your eyes. The single most useful one for a bowler — bowling demands thoracic rotation, and desks take it away.
4
Thread the Needle8 / side
Hands and knees. Slide the right arm under the left, shoulder and temple to the floor. More thoracic rotation, different angle.
5
90/90 Hip Switches8 / side
Sit with both knees bent 90° — one leg in front, one out to the side. Rotate both knees to the other side without using hands. Slow. This is the hip mobility that carries a bowling stride.
6
Kneeling Hip Flexor45 s / side
Half-kneeling. Squeeze the glute of the kneeling leg and tuck the pelvis under before leaning forward — otherwise you just arch your lower back and stretch nothing. Tight hip flexors from sitting are a common contributor to back stiffness.
7
Adductor Rock-Backs10 reps
Hands and knees, one leg straight out to the side, foot flat. Rock hips back toward the heel. Groin — matters for the sideways push in bowling and sprinting.
8
Standing Hamstring45 s / side
Heel on a low step, leg straight, hinge from the hips with a flat back — not a rounded spine. Rounding here loads the lower back, which is exactly what you're protecting.
9
Wall Calf Stretch45 s / side, ×2Running
Hands on wall, back leg straight, heel pressed down — that's the gastroc. Then repeat with the back knee slightly bent — that's the soleus. Do both. Tight calves are the most common source of running niggles.
10
Doorway Pec Stretch45 s / side
Forearm on the door frame, elbow at shoulder height, step through and rotate away. Counters all the pressing on Wednesday.
11
Child's Pose with Side Reach30 s / side
From child's pose, walk both hands to the left, then the right. Lats and the side of the torso.
12
Dead Hang2 × 30 s
Hang from a bar, shoulders relaxed, feet off or lightly touching. Decompresses the spine and opens the shoulders. Stop early if grip or shoulders complain.
13
Easy walk30–45 min
Outdoors. Not a workout — take the toddler.
14
Weigh · waist · photos · cookmorning
Log in the Today tab. Photos every second Sunday. Portion the week's chicken.
No lifting, no running. Six active days only works if this one is genuinely easy.

Stretching · the rules

Hold time30–45 s per stretch
Intensitymild tension · never pain
Breathingnormal · don't hold breath
Bouncingnever
Before trainingdynamic only — swings, skips
After / Sundaystatic holds
Why the split: long static holds immediately before lifting or sprinting temporarily reduce force output. Dynamic movement before, static holds after or on Sunday.
If a stretch produces sharp pain, pins and needles, or pain that travels down a limb — stop. That's not tightness and it's worth a physio's opinion, not more stretching.

Six weeks · honest expectation

At 0.4–0.6 kg per week you'll drop roughly 3–4 kg, landing near 18–19% body fat — waist visibly tighter, shoulders more obvious against it, upper abs showing in good light.

You should also notice the run getting easier at the same effort, and less gassed at the end of a bowling spell. That's the part that lasts longer than the photo.

Coaching guidance built around your stated constraints — post-cholecystectomy fat tolerance, reflux, back protection. Not medical advice, and not a substitute for your doctor on vitamin D dosing, fat tolerance or persistent gut symptoms. New or worsening back pain, joint pain from running, reflux that won't settle, or any concerning symptom: stop and get it looked at.
